# Assessment Framework

Each intervention follows the same 18-step evidence workflow.

1. Define the intervention.
2. Reconstruct policy or programme logic.
3. Define evaluation questions.
4. Define indicators.
5. Map required data.
6. Find or acquire data.
7. Assess data quality.
8. Clean and standardise data.
9. Integrate datasets.
10. Analyse implementation.
11. Analyse outcomes.
12. Assess attribution or impact.
13. Assess equity.
14. Assess efficiency and value for money.
15. Document limitations.
16. Produce intervention findings.
17. Add the intervention to the Tracker.
18. Archive reusable methodology and evidence records.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. Anonymised programme administrative records, audited expenditure, and original survey microdata.
2. Published baseline, midterm, endline, and independent-evaluation reports with methods and sampling detail.
3. Official implementation and results reports.
4. Official contextual statistics, such as production, prices, rainfall, and market data.
5. Secondary commentary.

Lower-tier evidence can document design, implementation, or context. It should not be used to make a causal impact claim when stronger evidence is unavailable.

## Claim discipline

- **Reported**: a programme or official document states a value.
- **Descriptive**: an observed trend is documented without a counterfactual.
- **Attributable**: a credible design estimates the programme's causal contribution.
- **Not assessable**: required data or methods are not available. This does not mean that there was no effect.

## Minimum fields for an impact assessment

An impact assessment should normally have anonymised beneficiary IDs, dates of exposure, intervention type/intensity, location, relevant outcomes, baseline and follow-up measures, and a valid comparison group or other defensible counterfactual. It should also document sampling, missing data, and key contextual shocks.
